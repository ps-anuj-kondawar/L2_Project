Benzene
Benzene (C6H6) is classified as a Group 1 human carcinogen by IARC and OSHA.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 1 ppm TWA (8 hour time weighted average).
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 5 ppm over 15 minutes.
Action Level: 0.5 ppm (triggers mandatory air monitoring and medical surveillance).
Use in open formulation systems is heavily restricted. Maximum allowable
concentration in open system laboratory formulations is 0.1% by volume.
NTP: Known to be a Human Carcinogen (15th Report on Carcinogens).
California Proposition 65 WARNING: known to cause cancer and reproductive harm.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1028.

Acetone
Acetone (C3H6O) is a flammable solvent classified as a skin and eye irritant.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 1000 ppm TWA.
Acetone is not classified as a carcinogen. Flash point is -20 degrees C.
Requires adequate ventilation in laboratory settings. Storage must be away
from ignition sources.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Toluene
Toluene (C7H8) is a flammable aromatic solvent and a reproductive hazard.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 200 ppm TWA.
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 300 ppm ceiling value (must not be exceeded).
Prolonged exposure may cause central nervous system effects and reproductive harm.
Pregnant workers must avoid exposure due to documented fetal harm.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-2.

Methanol
Methanol (CH3OH) is a highly toxic alcohol. It is metabolised to formaldehyde
and formic acid in the human body, causing severe neurological damage.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 200 ppm TWA.
Skin absorption is a significant secondary exposure route; gloves are mandatory.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Isopropanol
Isopropanol (C3H8O), also known as IPA, is a common laboratory solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 400 ppm TWA.
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 500 ppm.
Low toxicity compared to methanol. Eye and skin irritant at high concentrations.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Chloroform
Chloroform (CHCl3) is a trihalomethane solvent and probable human carcinogen.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 50 ppm Ceiling limit (must not be exceeded at any time).
IARC Group 2A: Probably Carcinogenic to Humans.
NTP: Reasonably Anticipated to be a Human Carcinogen (15th Report on Carcinogens).
Causes central nervous system depression, liver damage, and kidney failure.
Must be handled strictly inside a certified chemical fume hood with Viton gloves.
California Proposition 65 WARNING: known to cause cancer.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-2.

Formaldehyde
Formaldehyde (CH2O) is classified as a Group 1 human carcinogen and sensitiser.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 0.75 ppm TWA.
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 2 ppm. Action Level: 0.5 ppm.
Severe respiratory tract hazard and skin sensitiser. Air monitoring required.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1048.

Xylene
Xylene (C8H10), mixed isomers, is a flammable aromatic solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 100 ppm TWA.
Eye and respiratory tract irritant; narcotic at high concentrations.
Store in dedicated flammable storage cabinets away from oxidisers.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Hexane
n-Hexane (C6H14) is a neurotoxic aliphatic hydrocarbon solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 500 ppm TWA.
Causes peripheral neuropathy and numbness in extremities upon chronic exposure.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Ethyl Acetate
Ethyl Acetate (C4H8O2) is a flammable ester solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 400 ppm TWA.
Vapours may cause drowsiness and dizziness. Irritating to eyes.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Dichloromethane
Dichloromethane (CH2Cl2), also known as Methylene Chloride, is a volatile halogenated solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 25 ppm TWA.
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 125 ppm. Action Level: 12.5 ppm.
IARC Group 2A: Probably Carcinogenic to Humans. NTP: Reasonably Anticipated to be a Human Carcinogen.
Cardiac sensitization hazard. Metabolised to carbon monoxide in vivo.
California Proposition 65 WARNING: known to cause cancer.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1052.

Diethyl Ether
Diethyl Ether (C4H10O) is an extremely flammable ether solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 400 ppm TWA.
Forms explosive organic peroxides upon prolonged storage and air exposure.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Sodium Hydroxide
Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH), caustic soda, is a strongly alkaline corrosive solid/solution.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 2 mg/m3 TWA.
Causes severe tissue burns and permanent eye damage. Face shield and rubber apron required.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Sulfuric Acid
Sulfuric Acid (H2SO4) is a strong corrosive mineral acid and oxidizer.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 1 mg/m3 TWA (airborne acid mist).
Causes severe skin burns and eye damage. Do not add water to concentrated acid.
IARC Group 1: Acid mists from sulfuric acid are classified as carcinogenic to humans.
NTP: Known to be a Human Carcinogen (15th Report on Carcinogens, acid mist).
California Proposition 65 WARNING: acid mist known to cause cancer.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Hydrochloric Acid
Hydrochloric Acid (HCl), muriatic acid, is a corrosive mineral acid gas/solution.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 5 ppm Ceiling.
Inhalation of fumes causes severe respiratory irritation and pulmonary edema.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Ammonia
Ammonia (NH3) is a corrosive alkaline gas and aqueous solution.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 50 ppm TWA.
Pungent suffocating odor. Corrosive to eyes, skin, and respiratory system.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Hydrogen Peroxide
Hydrogen Peroxide (H2O2) solution (>8%) is a strong oxidiser and corrosive liquid.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 1 ppm TWA.
May cause fire or explosion in contact with combustible materials.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Acetonitrile
Acetonitrile (C2H3N) is a toxic, flammable polar solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 40 ppm TWA.
Metabolised to hydrogen cyanide in vivo. Handle in fume hood with nitrile/neoprene gloves.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.

Dimethyl Sulfoxide
Dimethyl Sulfoxide (C2H6OS), DMSO, is a powerful dipolar aprotic solvent.
OSHA has not established a Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL) for DMSO.
ACGIH has not established a Threshold Limit Value (TLV) for DMSO.
DMSO rapidly penetrates human skin and transports dissolved solutes directly into the bloodstream,
making it particularly dangerous when used as a vehicle for toxic or pharmacological compounds.
Handle with nitrile gloves. Avoid skin contact with contaminated solutions.
Source: AIHA WEEL / OSHA Hazard Communication. No 29 CFR 1910.1000 PEL established.

Tetrahydrofuran
Tetrahydrofuran (C4H8O), THF, is a flammable cyclic ether solvent.
Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL): 200 ppm TWA.
Short-Term Exposure Limit (STEL): 250 ppm. Forms explosive peroxides on exposure to air.
Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1000 Table Z-1.
